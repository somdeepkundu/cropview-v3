Field guide

Knowing a Shevga
  • Leaves feathery, two to three times divided, 30–60 cm long; leaflets small and oval, 1–2 cm, paler beneath.
  • Bark pale grey, soft and corky; branches brittle.
  • Flowers creamy white, fragrant, in loose sprays. Pods long, ribbed “drumsticks”, 25–45 cm.
  • Flush is the new soft, lime-green leaves. Older leaves are darker and firmer.
Each tree, each visit
  1. Open the tree: scan the tag or pick it from the list (nearest first).
  2. Fix where it stands — one of the three ways on the tree card.
  3. Photos, in the same order every time: whole tree from 8–15 m, the tag, the trunk, the canopy looking up, the leaves, flowers or pods.
  4. Fill in leaves, flowers, pods and the sample bag, then Save.
Direction and GPS on campus
  • Compass off? Tap it and draw a slow figure-8 with the phone. DISTURBED means something metal nearby is bending it: a parked car, railings, a pole. Step a few metres away.
  • No magnetic phone cases or mounts — they bend every reading.
  • GPS under a canopy is often ± 15 m or worse. Lock at the trunk anyway, then add whole-tree photos from two open sides: their directions cross at the tree.
  • Picture frozen? Tap ↻ (top right).
  • Before leaving, export the ZIP on the Trees tab.
